## 1. Business models of open-source dev-infra companies

**GitLab (open core, public: GTLB).** FY2026 (ended Jan 31, 2026): revenue $955.2M (+26%), gross margin 87%, net loss $56.0M, operating-cash-flow margin 24%, 10,682 customers >$5K ARR, 1,456 >$100K, 155 >$1M, net retention 118%, ~2,580 staff in 60 countries (10-K: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1653482/000162828026018731/gtlb-20260131.htm). Q4 FY26 GAAP op margin (7)%, non-GAAP 17%; $400M buyback authorized. Q2 FY27 (Jul 31, 2026): revenue $286.3M (+21%), GAAP gross margin 84% (down from 88% — AI/cloud COGS), GAAP op margin (20)%, non-GAAP 15%, NRR 117%, FY27 guide $1.129–1.133B; CEO Bill Staples (8-K: https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001653482/000162828026059820/gitlab-ex99120260731fy27.htm). Growth has decelerated 26% -> 21%. M&A: Reuters reported a sale exploration with Datadog interest (Jul 2024); renewed reports Oct 2025 of a Datadog bid possibly >$60/share with Morgan Stanley financing (https://www.fool.com/investing/breakfast-news/2025/10/17/breakfast-news-ddog-mulls-gitlab-takeover/). No deal appears in any filing through Q2 FY27 — treat "GitLab acquired" as NOT happened as of today. Model per 10-K: features used mainly by individual contributors are open source (CE, MIT); features valued by managers/directors/executives are "source-available subject to a proprietary software license" (EE). Named competitors: Microsoft/GitHub, Atlassian, JFrog, Harness. Lesson: buyer-based open core scaled to ~$1B, but gross margin is eroding with AI, and the model is now explicitly rejected by sovereignty buyers (see §5).

**Sourcegraph.** Took its code private on Aug 22, 2024; ended Cody Free/Pro/Enterprise Starter July 23, 2025; spun out the AI agent Amp as an independent company Dec 2025 (Amp claims profitability); Sourcegraph now sells a single Enterprise plan "from $16K" with no self-serve tier; ~138 employees (Tracxn, Aug 2026; UNVERIFIED) (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sourcegraph, https://tessl.io/blog/sourcegraph-spins-out-ai-coding-agent-amp-as-a-standalone-company). Lesson: closing source bought nothing visible; the company shrank to enterprise-only code search.

**HashiCorp -> BSL -> IBM; OpenTofu.** Terraform/Vault/etc. moved to BSL 1.1 Aug 2023; OpenTofu fork (Aug 2023, renamed Sep 2023) is Linux Foundation-managed and still shipping (1.13.1, Oct 1, 2026); HashiCorp's April 2024 cease-and-desist alleging BSL code copying was denied by OpenTofu with no public outcome (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OpenTofu). IBM closed the HashiCorp acquisition Feb 2025 (price ~$6.4B — UNVERIFIED here). BSL remains; no OSI option returned.

**Redis -> Valkey -> AGPL.** Redis left BSD for RSALv2/SSPLv1 on Mar 20, 2024; Linux Foundation forked Valkey; Redis added AGPLv3 with Redis 8 on May 1, 2025 (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Server_Side_Public_License). Secondary sources claim Valkey "83% enterprise adoption" and AWS migrated "millions" of ElastiCache nodes (UNVERIFIED; https://www.buildmvpfast.com/blog/valkey-vs-redis-open-source-fork-migration-2026). No visible reverse migration after the AGPL return.

**Elastic.** SSPL/Elastic License Jan 2021; AWS forked OpenSearch; Elastic added AGPLv3 Aug 29, 2024; OpenSearch Software Foundation formed under LF Sep 16, 2024. Same pattern: fork survived, relicensing reversed. **MongoDB** stays SSPL (since Oct 2018; OSI submission withdrawn 2019; Debian/RHEL/Fedora dropped packages; OSI called it "fauxpen" in 2021).

**Sentry / FSL / Fair Source.** FSL: do anything "except undermine its producer"; each version converts to Apache-2.0/MIT after 2 years (https://fsl.software/). Fair Source adopters listed on fair.io/companies: Sentry, GitButler, Keygen, PowerSync, CodeCrafters (Aug 2024), Typebot, Qlty, Pythagora, Ayon, Chartbrew, Sourcebot, Tuist, Liquibase (Sep 30, 2025) — 13 total. Sentry self-reports 100K+ orgs and $100M+ ARR (https://blog.sentry.io/open-source). Takeaway: Fair Source is a small but real club; works for SaaS-first companies; it is explicitly "not open source", which disqualifies it with OSI-policy public buyers.

**Grafana.** Apache-2.0 -> AGPLv3 in Apr 2021 for Grafana/Loki/Tempo; Enterprise edition is a separate proprietary binary (https://devclass.com/2021/04/21/all-change-grafana-switches-core-open-source-projects-from-apache-to-agplv3-licensing/). Enterprise legal teams grumbled (forum posts) but Grafana became ubiquitous anyway. Grafana Labs financials: UNVERIFIED.

**Supabase (Apache-2.0, cloud-first).** $500M Series F at $10.5B (Jun 2026, GIC lead); est. ARR ~$170M May 2026, up from ~$101M end-2025; Series E at ~$5B Oct 2025 (https://sacra.com/c/supabase/). **PostHog (MIT core, usage pricing).** $70M Series D at $920M (Jun 2025, Stripe), $75M Series E at $1.4B (Oct 2025, Peak XV); est. ARR $57.5M Feb 2026 (+99% YoY); internal target $100M ARR by end-2026 (https://sacra.com/c/posthog/, https://posthog.com/handbook/strategy/overview). PostHog killed Kubernetes/Helm self-hosting May 31, 2023: only 3.5% of users were on K8s but they consumed "an outsized amount" of infra-team time; kept MIT docker-compose "hobby" and pushed Cloud (https://posthog.com/blog/sunsetting-helm-support-posthog).

**n8n (Sustainable Use License, not OSI).** SUL since Mar 2022 ("fair-code"); $180M Series C at $2.5B (Oct 9, 2025, Accel; total $240M); ARR >$40M by Aug 2025 (~10x YoY); SAP secondary marked it at $5.2B (May 12, 2026) (https://community.n8n.io/t/announcing-our-180m-series-c-funding/202535, https://sacra.com/c/n8n/). Takeaway: the "not real open source" controversy (HN threads since 2019) did not stop hypergrowth — because the buyer is business users, not FOSS-policy procurement.

**Cal.com.** As of Oct 2026 the github.com/calcom/cal.com README presents "Cal.diy" as "100% MIT-licensed" with enterprise features (Teams, Orgs, SSO/SAML, Insights, Workflows) removed, explicitly contrasted with Cal.com's "Open Core" commercial product (https://github.com/calcom/cal.com). Funding/revenue: UNVERIFIED. **Coolify (Apache-2.0, bootstrapped, solo founder).** Feb 2025: $15.7K gross/month ($10.5K cloud + $5.2K donations), $2.8K costs; 2026 est. $17–30K MRR, ~3,400 cloud customers (https://x.com/heyandras/status/1901894087604916396, https://opencollective.com/coollabsio). **Plausible (AGPLv3, bootstrapped).** 10 people, >21,000 paying subscribers, crossed $1M ARR 2022, "subscription revenue is the only revenue we have" (https://plausible.io/about).

**Mattermost.** ~$33.1M 2024 revenue est., $70M raised, last round 2019 (https://getlatka.com/companies/mattermost); pricing is quote-only; Enterprise adds FIPS 140-3 image, STIG image, HA, compliance export; "Enterprise Advanced" adds air-gapped/DDIL (https://mattermost.com/pricing/). License structure (MIT Team Edition vs source-available EE) — conflicting sources, UNVERIFIED. **Zulip (Apache-2.0, Kandra Labs).** Cloud Standard $6.67/user/mo annual, Plus $10; self-hosted Basic $3.50, Business $6.67 (25-user min), Enterprise quote; free for OSS (https://zulip.com/plans/). **Penpot (MPL, Kaleidos, Madrid).** Funding UNVERIFIED.

**Gitea Ltd vs Forgejo/Codeberg e.V.** Gitea forked from Gogs Nov 2016. In Oct 2022 maintainers Lunny Xiao and Matti Ranta founded Gitea Ltd and moved gitea.io/gitea.com domains and the trademark to it "without knowledge or approval of the community"; an open letter asking for community stewardship was rejected; Codeberg forked Forgejo (Oct–Dec 2022), hard fork since early 2024 (https://christiantietze.de/posts/2022/11/gitea-ltd-takes-over-open-source-community-pushes-back/, https://forgejo.org/compare-to-gitea/). Gitea Enterprise/Cloud are sold by CommitGo Inc. ("Gitea is open source, with leadership and commercial support from CommitGo"); Gitea requires a copyright-assignment CLA (per Forgejo) (https://about.gitea.com/pricing/). Forgejo: GPLv3+ from v9.0 (Oct 2024), no registered trademarks, domains held by Codeberg e.V., governance independent of Codeberg (https://forgejo.org/faq/). What backfired: a silent trademark grab cost Gitea its community, Fedora, the Dutch government and Codeberg; the fork now out-adopts the original in the public sector.

**Patterns.** (a) Every relicensing to source-available since 2021 produced a surviving LF-backed fork (OpenSearch, OpenTofu, Valkey) and two reversals to AGPL (Elastic, Redis); AGPL is now the consensus "protective but OSI" license. (b) Revenue follows cloud, not the license: Supabase/PostHog/n8n are cloud-dominant regardless of MIT/Apache/SUL. (c) Bootstrapped FOSS SaaS (Plausible, Coolify) is profitable but caps at low single-digit $M.

## 2. License choice analysis for a new forge

- **MIT/Apache-2.0**: maximum adoption, zero legal friction, but no fork protection; Gitea (MIT) was forked and out-governed; GitLab CE (MIT) works because EE is the moat. Apache-2.0 adds an explicit patent grant (CNCF requires Apache-2.0 by default).
- **AGPL-3.0**: protects against a hyperscaler running your forge as a service without contributing. Running unmodified AGPL software internally imposes no disclosure obligation; §13 triggers only on modified versions offered over a network. Legal-department FUD is real (Grafana forum: legal said "out of the question unless they are modifying"; secondhand claims of Google's internal AGPL ban: https://thebuild.com/blog/the-agpl-radioactive-by-design/), but actual practice shows Grafana, Elastic, Redis 8, Plausible, Zitadel (AGPL-3.0 with Apache/MIT exceptions: https://github.com/zitadel/zitadel), Mattermost deployed across enterprises. Dual-licensing (AGPL + commercial license for the AGPL-averse) requires owning copyright, i.e., a CLA.
- **GPLv3+ (Forgejo)**: chosen over AGPL/EUPL "to prevent a takeover from a for-profit company"; AGPL/EUPL "suggested… no agreement has been reached"; EUPL is incompatible with "or later" GPL (https://forgejo.org/faq/, https://codeberg.org/forgejo/discussions/issues/201). Forgejo keeps its API swagger file MIT for interoperability (https://forgejo.org/2024-10-release-v9-0/). Rationale: accept copyleft contributors (decided June 2023), reuse copyleft deps, protect users from lock-in forks, Git itself is GPLv2 (https://forgejo.org/2024-08-gpl/).
- **MPL-2.0**: file-level copyleft; Penpot, Earthly (BSL->MPL). Weak protection against SaaS competitors.
- **FSL/BSL**: not OSI; excluded by OSI-policy public buyers (Dutch, German openCode "all projects comply with OSI standards"); fine for SaaS-first.
- **Open core with /ee directory (GitLab, Cal.com)**: proven to $1B, but Fedora rejected GitLab because "GitLab CE contributions are accepted only for its Enterprise Edition" and issue features were paywalled (https://communityblog.fedoraproject.org/fedora-chooses-forgejo/), and the Netherlands rejected GitLab "due to its open-core business model" (https://ascii.co.uk/news/article/news-20260514-4042f74b/dutch-government-launches-sovereign-forgejo-instance-abandon).
- **CLA vs DCO**: DCO = inbound-equals-outbound, relicensing requires every contributor's consent (credible anti-rug-pull commitment); CLA = relicensing/dual-licensing freedom plus patent grant but is the Gitea/HashiCorp/Redis fear trigger. Linux kernel and GitLab use DCO; Apache/Google use CLAs (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Developer_Certificate_of_Origin).
- **Trademark**: the single most important asset — the Gitea dispute was entirely about domains/trademark; Earthly kept its trademark and forced forks to rename (https://earthly.dev/blog/shutting-down-earthfiles-cloud/); CNCF requires trademark transfer to LF on acceptance (sandbox doc fetch failed — UNVERIFIED detail). Publish a Model-Trademark-Guidelines-style policy (nominative use OK, forks must rename, no "official hosting" implication).
- **Foundations/funding**: OpenInfra joined LF Mar 2025, keeping its own board/budget (https://openinfra.org/blog/openinfra-joins-linux-foundation/); Keycloak is CNCF Incubating since Apr 10, 2023; Codeberg e.V. (Berlin) holds Forgejo's domains and had 1,691 paying members in its 2026 budget post and 1,700+ members/444,143 users/737,292 projects on the homepage (https://blog.codeberg.org/codebergs-budget-of-2026.html, https://codeberg.org/). Sovereign Tech Agency (SPRIND subsidiary, funded by Germany's digital ministry) invested >€23M in ~60 projects 2022–2024 and €85,600 in "Forgejo Federation" for 2025–2026 (https://www.sovereign.tech/tech/forgejo-federation). Forgejo has filed to NLnet (Apr 2025), Sovereign Tech Fund (Aug 2024) and OTF (May 2024) (https://codeberg.org/forgejo/sustainability/issues/72).

Observations: (1) Enterprises demonstrably pay $21 (GitHub) to $29 (GitLab Premium) per seat for the collaboration tier and ~$99 for a security/compliance tier; a self-hosted challenger can anchor at $12–19 (Gitea's $19 ceiling; undercut GitHub Enterprise's $21). (2) AI is now a usage-metered add-on on both incumbents ($12–24 of credits/seat on GitLab; AI Credits on GitHub) — margin pressure visible in GitLab's gross margin drop from 88% to 84%. (4) Single-tenant managed ("Dedicated") is the enterprise/government path for GitLab, Mattermost (Cloud Enterprise) and Ona (runners in your cloud). (5) Government: self-hosted software needs no FedRAMP; the hosting agency carries it — a structural advantage over SaaS.

## 4. Enterprise adoption checklist (evidence-backed)

- **Identity/governance**: SAML SSO, SCIM provisioning, RBAC, audit log + API/SIEM export are the line items GitHub Enterprise ($21) and Gitea Enterprise ($9.50–19) gate on; Buildkite gates audit log/SCIM at Enterprise only. These define the paid tier in every forge.
- **Data residency**: GitHub only offers EU and Australia (Azure); GitLab Dedicated sells data isolation/residency. Open gap: any-region, customer-cloud deployment.
- **Air-gapped / FIPS / STIG**: Mattermost prices these at its top two tiers (FIPS 140-3 image, STIG image, DDIL/air-gapped). Expect the same from a forge buyer in defense/finance.
- **SOC 2 Type II / ISO 27001**: Forgejo criticizes Gitea for "spending resources on a SOC2 audit for its SaaS while critical vulnerabilities awaited a release" — i.e., SaaS buyers demand SOC 2 even from tiny vendors. Cost benchmarks could not be verified (UNVERIFIED; typical industry range $30K–$100K+ year one incl. tooling, 3–12 month observation window).
- **FedRAMP**: Rev 5 Moderate estimates $800K–$2M and 18–26 months; "FedRAMP 20x" vendors claim $100–300K and 3–6 months with Moderate pilot running through 2026 (vendor sources: https://fedrampcost.com/, https://www.paramify.com/blog/fedramp-paths). GitLab Dedicated for Government advertises "FedRAMP compliance" (level UNVERIFIED). Advice from consultants: below ~$5M federal pipeline, use StateRAMP, 20x, or a prime contractor.
- **EU Cyber Resilience Act (Reg. 2024/2847)**: manufacturers must report actively exploited vulnerabilities/severe incidents via ENISA's Single Reporting Platform since **Sep 11, 2026** (24h early warning, 72h notification, 14-day final report); open-source stewards' Art. 24(3) duties and full application (SBOM, conformity, CE marking) from **Dec 11, 2027** (https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/cra-reporting). A commercially supported forge sold into the EU is a "manufacturer", not a steward.
- **US**: EO 14028 -> OMB M-22-18/M-23-16 secure-software attestation (SSDF) via CISA's RSAA; CISA's form page is now marked archived "may not reflect current policy" (https://www.cisa.gov/secure-software-attestation-form); 2025 changes via EO 14144/14306 UNVERIFIED.
- **Procurement**: security questionnaires, DPA/GDPR, support SLAs with credits (Buildkite 99.95%), roadmap commitments and source escrow — open source makes escrow moot, a selling point.
- **What actually blocks self-hosted forge adoption**: (a) CI — "CI is described as a major blocker for organizations moving to Forgejo" (HN thread on Dutch gov: https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=45929247); (b) operational complexity of distributed stacks (PostHog's 3.5%/outsized-support finding; GitLab's "8x RAM" vs Gitea in comparisons); (c) feature gaps: Forgejo Projects "very rudimentary", no auto-merge, accessibility (Fomantic UI) flagged by Fedora; (d) Copilot/AI bundling on GitHub; (e) fear of abandonment — Gitpod archived its self-hosted repo Apr 24, 2026 after the Ona pivot (https://www.theregister.com/software/2025/09/03/gitpod-rebrands-as-ona-now-an-ai-driven-dev-platform/).

## 5. The sovereignty wave

- **Netherlands**: code.overheid.nl, Forgejo-based, run by the Interior Ministry's OSPO, soft-launched ~Apr 24–27, 2026, modelled on Germany's opencode.de and the EC's code.europa.eu; the ministry must legally publish source "on a place that [it owns]"; GitHub excluded as proprietary, GitLab rejected for open core; Forgejo roadmap "way more aligned", co-development with community; pilot with "a few parties", reported plan to scale to all agencies within a year (https://interoperable-europe.ec.europa.eu/collection/open-source-observatory-osor/news/dutch-alternative-sharing-public-code, https://www.opensourceforu.com/2026/04/dutch-government-backs-forgejo-for-sovereign-open-source-github-alternative/). No budget figures published.
- **Germany**: openCode.de = ZenDiS-operated **GitLab** instance (gitlab.opencode.de) for public administration; >8,000 users, 3,000 projects, ~200 new/month (Aug 2025); OSI-license mandatory; badge program since 2025; openDesk office suite as flagship (https://opencode.de/en, https://allthingsopen.org/articles/zendis-opendesk-opencode-public-sector-open-source). Sovereign Tech Agency funds maintainers (€85.6K to Forgejo federation). Schleswig-Holstein: LibreOffice/Open-Xchange migration since 2024, "tens of millions" savings claimed.
- **Denmark**: Ministry of Digitalisation moving to LibreOffice (2025); Copenhagen and Aarhus phasing out Microsoft (https://therecord.media/denmark-digital-agency-microsoft-digital-independence).
- **EU**: code.europa.eu is GitLab ("Code development platform for open source projects from the European Union institutions", https://code.europa.eu/explore). EuroStack is still a proposal: €10B first-step fund, ~€300B by 2035, "EuroStack Challenge" competition (https://euro-stack.com/blog/2025/5/eurostack-white-paper, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EuroStack).
- **Community anchor**: Fedora Council unanimously (+9) chose Forgejo over GitLab on Dec 24, 2024; migration began Jan 2025, dist-git first (https://communityblog.fedoraproject.org/fedora-chooses-forgejo/). Hetzner now markets "Sovereignty starts with hosting it yourself" with Forgejo tutorials (https://www.hetzner.com/blog/community-owned-git-hosting-with-forgejo/).
- **China**: Gitee (OSChina, Shenzhen, 2013), MIIT-selected in 2020 to build the national independent code platform; mandatory manual pre-publication code review since May 18, 2022; ~8–10M users, 10M+ repos (self-reported) (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gitee). **Russia**: GitFlic (launched Dec 2021), in the national software registry, marketed to sanctioned universities/companies (https://habr.com/ru/companies/astralinux/articles/1073754/). **India**: 2014 OSS-preference policy, 2015 open-source-code policy, OpenForge.gov.in; no 2025–26 forge-specific change found; Jan 2026 proposal for smartphone source-code access (https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2024/02/policy_on_adoption_of_oss.pdf). **Brazil/LatAm**: no evidence found (UNVERIFIED).
- **Pattern**: public bodies that moved before 2024 chose GitLab (Germany, EC, France's many gitlab instances); those deciding in 2025–26 chose Forgejo (Netherlands, Fedora) explicitly because of open core and contribution policy. Dollar figures are tiny (€85.6K grants) — this wave buys with policy, not budgets, but it sets the reference architecture for European enterprises.

## 6. Go-to-market lessons

- **PLG + open source funnel**: PostHog's handbook: "customers buy from us rather than being sold to", target $100M ARR by end-2026 at ~7% monthly growth; Supabase's cloud-first PLG reached ~$170M ARR; n8n: >$40M ARR with a non-OSI license because buyers are business users.
- **Self-host is a funnel, not a product**: PostHog cut K8s self-hosting (3.5% users, outsized support) but kept docker-compose; Gitpod abandoned open-source self-hosted, pivoted to Ona (Sep 2025), archived the self-hosted repo (Apr 2026), "alienating the community of non-commercial users" — and the pivot ended in an OpenAI deal (status conflicting, UNVERIFIED).
- **Earthly's post-mortem** (Apr 2025): enterprises adopted bottom-up but "wouldn't commit to company-wide deployments"; users got the value from the free tool and DIY'd around the paid Satellites; infra investors moved to AI, "less appetite for pure-play open-source moonshots"; forks must rename (trademark kept); users pointed to Dagger (https://earthly.dev/blog/shutting-down-earthfiles-cloud/).
- **Sourcegraph**: closing source then exiting the agent business shows a dev-tool company can't out-run the model vendors on AI; code understanding infra survived.
- **GitLab**: handbook-first all-remote (1,300 staff in 65 countries by 2020), YC W15 with $1.5M seed, $4M A (Sep 2015), $20M B and $20M C (2016), IPO Oct 14, 2021 raising $650M (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GitLab_Inc.).
- **Migration-as-wedge**: Gitea/Forgejo already import from GitHub/GitLab; Forgejo Actions is GitHub-Actions-compatible, and the Zig project reports runner development "faster than Gitea's act_runner"; CI compatibility was the blocker cited for Dutch migration.
- **Community infra**: Forgejo runs on its own forge (code.forgejo.org) with Weblate; Fedora uses Matrix office hours; Codeberg's governance via member votes (88–0–1 on 2025 budget).

## 7. Team/cost reality

- **Forgejo** operates on volunteer effort plus tiny budgets: Codeberg's 2025 Forgejo-related lines were €14,800 dedicated, €14,000 contract work, €3,000 temporary employment, €10,800 volunteer compensation for up to 12 people (€840/yr tax-free each) — about €42.6K total, plus the €85.6K STA grant; "employee delegation" (e.g., meissa GmbH) supplies paid contributors (https://codeberg.org/forgejo/sustainability/issues/72). Yet it ships quarterly releases, LTS, and v15.0 (Apr 16, 2026).
- **Gitea** forked Gogs in Nov 2016 because Gogs had a single maintainer; a handful of maintainers carried it to 28.x (Oct 6, 2026). **GitLab** started as one developer in 2011; a company in 2014; ~9 people at YC in 2015 (UNVERIFIED).
- **Funding rounds 2024–26 (verified)**: Blacksmith $3.5M seed + $10M A (GV, 2025), later rounds listed by Tracxn UNVERIFIED; Northflank ~$22M A (Nov 2024, total ~$25M); Railway Series B Jan 22, 2026 (~$120M total, UNVERIFIED); PostHog, Supabase, n8n as above. Graphite, Depot, Dagger, Harness rounds: UNVERIFIED (search budget exhausted).
- **What 1–5 people can ship in 12 months**: evidence says a differentiated layer, not a forge from scratch. Coolify = 1 person at ~$15K/month; Plausible = 10 people; Forgejo = ~dozen part-paid contributors maintaining a 100MB Go codebase with 5,000+ UI strings. Building git hosting, PRs, issues, CI, packages, SSO and migration tooling de novo is a multi-year effort.
- **Reusable components**: Forgejo (GPLv3+, mostly MIT files; API spec MIT) or Gitea (MIT, but CLA + CommitGo control); Gitaly (MIT, GitLab's git RPC layer); Zoekt (Apache-2.0, maintained by Sourcegraph since 2017, 1,991 commits: https://github.com/sourcegraph/zoekt); Woodpecker CI (Apache-2.0 Drone fork) or Forgejo Actions runner; Keycloak (Apache-2.0, CNCF Incubating since Apr 2023: https://www.cncf.io/projects/keycloak/), Zitadel (AGPL-3.0, same codebase cloud/self-host), Ory (Apache-2.0); Sigstore/SLSA for attestations.

## Recommended license, governance, business model and GTM — with reasoning

**License.** Server/core: **AGPL-3.0-only** (not GPL: a forge is network software; AGPL is the license Grafana, Elastic, Redis, Plausible and Zitadel converged on; it is OSI-approved so it passes Dutch/German/Fedora policy where FSL/SUL/open-core fail). Everything that must be embedded or integrated by others — API spec, SDKs, CLI, runner protocol, webhooks schema, migration tooling — **Apache-2.0** (Forgejo keeps its swagger MIT for exactly this reason; Apache adds a patent grant). If you build on Forgejo, AGPL-3.0 is GPLv3-compatible, so the combined work can be AGPL. **DCO, no CLA**: inbound=outbound makes a future rug-pull practically impossible, which is the exact fear Gitea/HashiCorp/Redis created; you give up dual-licensing, so compensate with services and hosting rather than a proprietary edition.

**Governance.** Company holds the trademark and domains initially (the Gitea lesson is to be transparent about this on day one, not that it is wrong); publish a trademark policy (nominative use allowed, forks must rename, no implied "official" hosting) and a written Stewardship Charter (no relicensing, no CLA, API spec stays permissive, data export always free, security advisories to everyone at the same time — Forgejo's explicit contrast with Gitea's customer-only advance notice). Pre-commit that if the company is acquired or wound down, trademark and domains transfer to a named non-profit (e.g., a Codeberg-style e.V. or a fiscal sponsor). Pursue CNCF/LF only once revenue exists (CNCF requires trademark transfer).

**Business model (in order).** 1) Hosted cloud with usage-metered CI/compute and AI credits (where all the revenue in this sector actually is); 2) Dedicated single-tenant in the customer's cloud/region (the GitLab Dedicated/Mattermost Cloud Enterprise pattern; satisfies residency and sidesteps FedRAMP); 3) Self-hosted Enterprise Subscription at $12–19/seat: signed FIPS/STIG builds, LTS backports, SLA support, compliance pack (SOC 2 report, SBOM/VEX, CRA reporting), SCIM/SIEM connectors — delivered as supported builds, not paywalled source; 4) public-sector support contracts; 5) marketplace at a 5% take (match GitHub). Do not sell "faster CI" as the core wedge (Earthly), and do not sell a proprietary EE directory (loses the sovereignty wave).
